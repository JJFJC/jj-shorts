# Gantry Arm — Step 1 simulation report

Generated by `sim/static_check.py` + `sim/report.py` from `params.py`. Re-run both after changing any parameter.

## Setup

- Model: `urdf/gantry_arm.urdf` and `mjcf/gantry_arm.xml` (generated). 2 prismatic (X 800 mm, Z 500 mm) + 5 revolute + gripper (2 mimic fingers).
- Arm: J2→TCP reach **450 mm** (upper 165, fore 150, wrist 55, coupler 16, tool 64).
- Payload 500 g at the TCP, 15 mm off the roll axis. Arm mass without payload 1.45 kg.
- **Masses come from the CAD**: every printed shell's volume × PETG density (`cad/out/mass_props.json`), plus a hardware allowance per body (`params.HARDWARE_G`) and the servos at their real positions. Re-run `cad/build_all.py` before the sim after any CAD change.
- Elbow: **2× STS3250** (`params.ELBOW_SERVOS`, confirmed).
- **Full reach** = shoulder 90° (arm horizontal), elbow 0, wrist 0, Z at top of travel. Static torques from MuJoCo `mj_inverse` (gravity + spring, zero velocity).
- **Worst case** = max |torque| over a sweep of shoulder −100…100°, elbow −140…140°, wrist −110…110° (10° steps), with and without payload.
- Servo ratings at 12 V: STS3215 stall 30 kg·cm (2.94 N·m), rated ~10 kg·cm; STS3250 stall 50 kg·cm (4.90 N·m), rated 16 kg·cm (1.57 N·m).
- Shoulder spring: ideal zero-free-length spring (extension spring + cable over a pulley), anchor a = 40 mm above J2, attach b = 60 mm up the upper arm. Stiffness swept 0–2 N/mm; **k = 0.9 N/mm** gives the lowest worst-case shoulder torque. Spring force 65 N with the arm horizontal, max 70 N.
- Assumed layout: the 2060 rail lies flat on the table, the 2040 column stands on the X carriage with its 40 mm faces toward ±Y, and the platform cantilevers +Y.

† J1 has a vertical axis, so gravity puts no torque on it. The number shown is the torque to accelerate the arm at 3 rad/s² at full reach. J1 must still carry a **4.6 N·m bending moment**, and that has to go into a bearing, not the servo shaft.

The gripper needs 10 N per finger (μ = 0.5, safety factor 2.0) through a 10 mm pinion.

Twin shoulder servos: with no spring and equal load sharing, J2 is at 47% of combined stall (over the 40% flag). If the split is uneven (the worse servo carries 1/(2×0.85) = 59% of the load), that servo is at 55% of its stall. The spring is what makes J2 safe.

## Servo compliance (forward sim, holding the full-reach pose for 3 s)

MuJoCo position actuators use the SO-101 measured STS3215 stiffness (kp = 17.8 N·m/rad, scaled by each servo's stall torque). That kp is the bus default P gain.

| Case | J2 err ° | J3 err ° | J4 err ° | TCP drop mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Default P gain, no spring | 4.40 | 2.25 | 2.84 | 53.0 |
| Default P gain, spring | 2.33 | 2.26 | 2.91 | 37.0 |
| 2× P gain (P_Coefficient 16→32), spring | 1.17 | 1.14 | 1.45 | 18.6 |
| Gravity feed-forward on goal, spring | -0.00 | -0.00 | -0.00 | 0.6 |

Feed-forward means: add τ_gravity / kp to each goal position, using this same model. It is near-perfect in sim because the model is exact. On real hardware, expect a few mm, limited by mass errors and the servo deadband (±1 count = 0.09°).

## Gantry structure (Z at top, arm at full reach, payload on)

Hanging mass on the Z carriage: 2.62 kg, CoM 294 mm in front of the column. Moment into the column: 7.5 N·m at a height of 700 mm.

| Contribution | TCP sag (mm) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2040 column bending (strong axis) | 0.92 | rotation 0.091° |
| Z carriage rocking on V-wheels | 0.18 – 0.90 | max wheel load 24 N (check against the wheel datasheet rating) |
| X carriage rocking on 2060 | 0.39 – 1.97 | max wheel load 43 N |
| PETG platform (ribbed, I = 181×10³ mm⁴) | 0.96 | bending stress 1.1 MPa (low, but PETG creeps under constant load) |
| PETG arm links (hollow 3 mm wall) | 2.04 | conservative |
| **Total elastic sag** | **4.5 – 6.8** | stiff vs. servo compliance |

**Wobble:** first natural frequency of the column with the platform and arm on it, Z at the top:

- Along X (2040 weak axis + X carriage rocking): 6.4 – 7.8 Hz. A 1000 mm/s² X move bends the column by 0.42 – 0.62 mm, and it rings at that frequency after the move.
- Along Y (strong axis + X carriage rocking across the rail): 6.0 – 10.8 Hz.
- Below ~15 Hz, it is visible in the wrist camera and couples with stepper mid-band resonance. The fix is cheap: an S-curve/jerk-limited X profile, a diagonal brace from the X carriage to the column, and preloaded (eccentric) wheels.

## Drives

- Z (T8×2): lift force 36 N → 27 mN·m to raise. The lowering torque is **+4.1 mN·m**, so the screw self-locks: lead angle 5.2° < friction angle 7.1° at μ = 0.12. 20 mm/s needs 600 rpm. A NEMA17 has plenty of torque there on 24 V, but only marginal on 12 V (torque falls off above ~500 rpm).
- X (GT2 20T): 4.0 kg moving, 1000 mm/s² + 8 N drag → 12 N, 77 mN·m at 300 rpm. Large margin.

## Not modelled

- URDF cannot express the shoulder spring; it is only in the MJCF (`tendon/shoulder_spring`).
- Dynamic loads from arm motion are not in the static numbers (except the J1 example). Expect +20–50% during fast moves.
- V-wheel stiffness is an estimate (300–1500 N/mm per wheel), so results are shown as a range.
- PETG creep, layer adhesion and print orientation are handled in Step 2 (print orientation per part).
